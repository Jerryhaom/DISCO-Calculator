How DISCO is calculated

DISCO measures the change in the reference correlation matrix after adding one individual's biomarker profile. The calculation uses stored aggregate statistics from the UK Biobank reference (n = 5,282); individual reference records are not required.

1. Transform and standardize

For raw biomarker value b, use t(b) = ln(b + 1) for CRP (mg/L) and t(b) = b for other markers. Then use the reference mean m and standard deviation s for that transformed marker.

$$x_{ij}=\frac{t_j(b_{ij})-m_j}{s_j}$$

xi is the individual's standardized biomarker vector.

2. Add the individual to the reference

Let μ be the reference mean on the standardized scale and A its centered cross-product matrix. The exact update for one additional individual is:

$$\begin{aligned}\delta_i&=x_i-\mu\\A_i^{+}&=A+\frac{n}{n+1}\delta_i\delta_i^{\top}\end{aligned}$$

n is the original reference size. This update accounts for the change in the reference mean.

3. Recalculate the correlations

Normalize each matrix entry by its diagonal terms to obtain the reference and updated Pearson correlations.

$$\begin{aligned}\rho_{jk}^{\mathrm{ref}}&=\frac{A_{jk}}{\sqrt{A_{jj}A_{kk}}}\\\rho_{jk}^{\mathrm{ref}+i}&=\frac{(A_i^{+})_{jk}}{\sqrt{(A_i^{+})_{jj}(A_i^{+})_{kk}}}\end{aligned}$$

Final DISCO score. Sum the weighted, squared changes in off-diagonal correlations, multiply by n², and take the natural logarithm.

$$\mathrm{DISCO}_i=\ln\!\left[n^2\sum_{j\ne k}w_{jk}\left(\rho_{jk}^{\mathrm{ref}}-\rho_{jk}^{\mathrm{ref}+i}\right)^2\right]$$

The weights are fixed UKB age-based weights. The calculation is algebraically exact given the stored aggregate statistics, up to parameter rounding and numerical precision.

Reference matrix and weight definitions

For standardized reference participants xr, the centered cross-product matrix is:

$$A=\sum_{r=1}^{n}(x_r-\mu)(x_r-\mu)^{\top}$$

Let aj be the correlation between standardized biomarker j and age in the UKB source cohort. Weights use absolute products of these correlations and are normalized over all ordered off-diagonal pairs:

$$w_{jk}=\frac{|a_ja_k|}{\sum_{u\ne v}|a_ua_v|}\quad(j\ne k),\qquad w_{jj}=0$$

Both (j, k) and (k, j) are included in the score sum. For the CHARLS and RuLAS relative comparisons, the matching 8- or 9-marker submatrices are used with the original weights, without renormalization.

Companion DM score. DM is the untransformed Mahalanobis distance, using the same standardized vector and a small diagonal regularization term.

$$\mathrm{DM}_i=\sqrt{\delta_i^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\delta_i},\qquad\Sigma=\frac{A}{n-1}+10^{-8}I$$

How the estimated percentile is obtained: the DISCO z-score is (DISCO − cohort mean) / cohort standard deviation. We interpolate between seven stored percentile thresholds (5, 10, 25, 50, 75, 90 and 95). Values outside those thresholds are reported as below the 5th or above the 95th percentile; their exact tail rank is unknown. For CHARLS and RuLAS, the comparison score is recalculated from the corresponding 8- or 9-marker subset to match the stored reference distribution; the full 10-marker scores remain above.

Interpretation: higher DISCO / DM indicates greater homeostatic dysregulation relative to the reference matrix. The cohort comparison describes relative standing, without adjusting for individual age or sex or estimating disease probability.

Analysis methods & cohort-specific covariates

Model and samples. Separate Cox models with Efron ties for DISCO and DM, using identical complete-case participants within each cohort. Only binary mortality status (0/1) and positive follow-up are accepted; lost-to-follow-up codes are excluded. No additional imputation is performed on the supplied cleaned biomarker inputs. Age and BMI are continuous; other covariates are categorical. “Fully adjusted” refers to the available adjustment set listed below, not every potential confounder.

  • UK Biobank: age, sex, BMI, ethnicity/race, education, smoking, alcohol use, physical activity.
  • NHANES: age, sex, BMI, ethnicity/race, education, smoking, alcohol use.
  • CHARLS: age, sex, BMI, education, smoking, alcohol use.
  • CLHLS: age, sex, BMI, education, smoking, alcohol use.
  • RuLAS: age, sex, BMI, education, smoking, alcohol use.

Analyzed waves. UK Biobank: Baseline 2006–2010; NHANES: Harmonized NHANES4 sample; CHARLS: Baseline 2011; CLHLS: Biomarker wave 2014; follow-up to 2018; RuLAS: Wave 2, 2016.

Scoring. Biomarkers are harmonized to the calculator's units, CRP is transformed with ln(CRP + 1), and the fixed UKB reference is applied. CHARLS and RuLAS use matching submatrices with the original weights, without renormalization. Their subset scores are distinct from full 10-marker scores. The calculator's relative z-scores and percentiles also use the matching cohort panel.

Estimation. Harrell's C is calculated from each full model's linear predictor. HR and C-index intervals are 95% Wald intervals; C-index bounds are restricted to [0, 1]. These models are unweighted, including NHANES; its complex survey design is not incorporated. Estimates have not been corrected for optimism or validated as a fixed mortality prediction model in held-out data. The calculator does not produce an absolute mortality risk.

Proportional-hazards check. Score-specific Schoenfeld-residual tests give p < 0.05 for UK Biobank DISCO (p = 8.72e-07); UK Biobank DM (p = 1.66e-16); NHANES DISCO (p = 1.74e-05); CHARLS DISCO (p = 0.012). These results indicate departures from proportional hazards; the displayed HRs summarize the fitted associations and warrant time-varying-effect sensitivity analyses. Other test p-values are in the downloadable results.

Provenance. Recomputed 2026-09-24 using the existing cohort biomarker and survival files and available covariates. Download aggregate results (JSON) · Reproduction script. Only aggregate results are published; participant-level inputs remain outside this repository.
